## Routes and tracks

### Route finder

- Access: Main menu > Route finder
- Keyboard shortcut: <kbd>g</kbd> <kbd>r</kbd>
- URL path: `/#tools=route-planner`

Toggles the route finder toolbar, which has: a transport/router selector, a mode selector, **Start** and **Finish** point pickers, a swap-endpoints button, and a close button.

Routing is based on a Freemap-hosted GraphHopper or third-party OSRM router.
A route can contain multiple midpoints.
Some midpoints may be marked as "manual," which activates manual routing as a user-drawn path for that route segment.

Where routing finds no way through — for the whole route, or for one leg of a multimodal one — the map draws a straight red dotted line between those waypoints instead. That stretch is treated as what it is rather than as a route: colorize leaves it alone (so the dotted line still shows), and the elevation profile draws a straight line across it rather than the terrain under it, which keeps the ridges it happens to cross out of the climb totals. The rest of the route is unaffected. A wholly failed route also reports a duration of zero, there being nothing routed to estimate one from; where only one leg failed, its straight line is given the pace of the legs that did route, so the total reads as an estimate rather than as nothing.

The **Start**/**Finish** (and midpoint) pickers offer: Select on the map, Your position, or Home position.

Supported vehicles (the transport dropdown also offers "Straight line" for fully manual routing):

- GraphHopper
  - Car
  - Car (4WD)
  - Car (avoid tolls) — experimental
  - Motorcycle
  - Bicycle
  - E-bike — experimental
  - Gravel bike — experimental
  - Racing bike
  - Mountain bike
  - Walking
  - Stroller / wheelchair — experimental
  - Hiking
  - Easy hiking — experimental
- OSRM
  - Car
  - Bicycle
  - Walking

The profiles marked experimental are new and still being tuned; the dropdown marks them with a flask. Bicycle, Walking and Car appear under both engines. The GraphHopper ones are the self-hosted router — weighted by the terrain model, so they avoid climbs, and the only ones that offer isochrones, round trips and the colorize modes that read the router's own data.

Routing modes:

- GraphHopper
  - Ordered
  - Roundtrip
  - Isochrones
- OSRM
  - Ordered
  - Visiting places
  - Visiting places (roundtrip)

Once a route is found the toolbar also offers:

- **Colorize by** (dropdown) — Inactive, then the modes in groups set off by dividers: Elevation, Steepness / Time, Speed, Heading / Surface, Smoothness, Road type, Track grade, Hiking difficulty, MTB difficulty, Waymark color (only modes the route has data for are shown; every mode except Elevation, Speed and Time is premium — badged with a gem and disabled without premium access); for the elevation profile and elevation/steepness colorize, premium users get every point sampled from the terrain model (high-resolution where available), while others keep the router's own elevation and only have missing values filled on demand. While a mode is active, the dropdown's first item is a **Show colorizing legend** checkbox (set off by a divider) that toggles a color-scale legend below the toolbar
  - **Steepness** is not a straight ramp: the colour follows an inverse-hyperbolic-sine curve bending at 1 %, so gentle grades get most of the palette and steep ones compress — a road route whose whole range is a couple of per cent was otherwise a uniformly black line. Where the palette **ends** is a setting, a slider in this dropdown right under the Steepness row (**Steepness range**, ±5 / 10 / 15 / 25 / 40 / 60 / 100 %, default 100 % = 45°, saved in local storage and shared with the track viewer and live tracking). No single value suits every route — measured against real ones, the widest that clamps almost nothing is 5 % for a road ride, 15 % for a car, 25 % on rolling hills and 60 % in the Tatras. The legend labels seven evenly spaced ticks with the grades they stand for, so it re-labels itself as the slider moves; colours mean the same grade on every route at a given setting
  - Surface, Smoothness, Road type, Track grade, the two difficulty ratings and Waymark color come from the router itself and so are offered on GraphHopper routes only. The four describing the way — including Track grade (OSM `tracktype`, grade 1 solid to grade 5 soft, what a forest road is actually like to ride or drive, which the surface alone doesn't say) and Smoothness (how rough the going is, grouped into Good / Intermediate / Bad / Very bad / Impassable) — are asked for on every profile; the difficulty scales are the profile's own, so Hiking difficulty (the SAC scale) appears on foot and hiking routes and MTB difficulty (the S scale) on the three bike ones. **Waymark color** is one mode whose meaning follows the profile the same way: it draws the route in the colors of the waymarked routes it is on, read from `osmc:symbol` or `colour` — the `route=hiking`/`route=foot` relations for a walking profile, the `route=bicycle`/`route=mtb` ones for a riding profile, since a walker and a rider follow different signs down the same lane. A car follows neither, so the mode is not offered for one. A way can carry both kinds and they can differ, which is why the profile picks rather than the mode. A stretch carrying several routes of different colors is painted in whichever of them the stretches around it also carry, so the line changes color only where the marking forces it to — yellow → yellow+blue → yellow stays yellow throughout — and where the change has to happen somewhere, the shared stretch goes to whichever trail the route follows further: purple 100 m → purple+yellow 200 m → yellow 200 m is drawn purple for 100 m and yellow for 400 m, not the other way round. A stretch on no marked route falls to a grey **Unmarked** category — named that rather than Unknown, since the router was asked about it and answered that there is no waymark — and it does not interrupt the above: the color carries across it and resumes after. A stretch nobody asked about is different and is drawn as **no data** (the thinner, dimmer neutral line) rather than counted as Unmarked: a car leg or an OSRM leg of a multimodal route is never sent either mask. In a multimodal route the walked and ridden parts are read as **separate networks** — they merely share a palette, so a red hiking trail never decides the color of a cycle route whose own marking runs blue. Where two colors run exactly as far as each other the change falls as late as it can, and where nothing else decides, the first of red, blue, green, yellow, black, orange, purple, white wins — the order the waymarks run from long-distance down to local. `other` is never in that contest: it stands for a color that could not be named, so it takes a stretch only where it is the sole marking there. These are the same eight colors the outdoor map draws its own trail lines in, with `other` its grey for anything else, so a route matches the trails under it. They paint named categories rather than a scale: the route is drawn in flat colors that change exactly where the value does, stretches the router has no value for get their own grey **Unknown** category (Waymark color is the exception described above, splitting that into Unmarked and no data), and the legend lists the categories the route actually holds with how far each one runs — asphalt vs. gravel vs. ground in kilometres, so it doubles as a summary of what the route is made of, down to how much of it nobody has mapped. A mode whose data is missing from the whole route is not offered at all, rather than offered and painting the line grey end to end
- **More** (⋮ menu) — **Elevation profile** (chart with total climb/descent; it leaves the menu for a button of its own on a toolbar with room for one, keeping its label where there is more room still), Convert to drawing (the route becomes one drawing line in its transport-mode color, plus a point per start/finish/stop; a long route asks for a maximum deviation first, offered already filled in with one derived from how many vertices it has), **Convert to Tracks and data** (the alternative being followed, plus the start/finish/stop markers, moves into the track viewer — one line per same-mode stretch, in the colors the map paints them — and the route planner is emptied and closed), **Route style…** (opens a modal with sliders for line width, line opacity, and marker opacity; saved in local storage), **Recompute route** (shown only while the route on screen is the one the open map has stored; asks the router again — a saved map draws the route it was saved with, and returns to it whenever the waypoints, mode and transport come back to what it was saved for, so this is the only way to pick up a changed routing graph; it marks the map as having unsaved changes), **Milestones** (Off / km / %, one choice — the markers count distance along the route or percentage of it), **Max routes to offer** (1–5, default 2, saved in local storage; shown only for point-to-point routing between exactly two waypoints under one transport, which is all either router computes alternatives for — a midpoint takes it away. It is a ceiling, not a promise: the router returns fewer where nothing else is both near enough in time and different enough to be worth offering, and on long inter-city routes that is often just one), and (premium; GraphHopper "Ordered" mode with 3+ points, single transport) **Optimize order** — reorders the waypoints to minimize total travel time, with variants: keep start fixed, keep start and finish fixed, round trip (return to start), or free (reorder all)
- **Delete** (🗑) — removes the result together with its start/finish/stop points. It appears as soon as the first point is placed, so a half-built route can be dropped as well

Selecting a start, finish or midpoint marker opens its own toolbar, carrying a ⋮ menu with the place actions for where that waypoint stands and an **Open in…** submenu below them, and 🗑 to remove just it.

Isochrone mode is set up in the mode menu itself: a time limit or a distance limit (whichever is filled in), the number of reachability bands (up to 5), and a **Travel to the point** checkbox that turns the rings around — they then cover where a journey to the point can start rather than where one from it can end, which differs wherever one-way streets and turn restrictions do.

An isochrone result offers the same **Delete** button and a reduced ⋮ menu with only Convert to drawing (one drawing polygon per reachability band, keeping any inner rings it has as holes, with the same simplification question when the rings are detailed enough to warrant it), **Convert to Tracks and data** (the rings and the start marker move into the track viewer as they are), and **Route style…** (the line width and opacity apply to the rings). It also counts as the "found route" source in the map data export and the map-to-document export, which always carry the route's start/finish/stop markers (named Start/Finish/Stop N in the data formats, which also carry the pin and its glyph — the play/stop icon, or the stop's number; on a rendered document the marker glyph speaks for itself, so no text label is drawn) and paint the route in the same transport-mode colors, width and opacity the map uses. Each ring is named after the limit it reaches — "Isochrone 30 min", or "Isochrone 5.00 km" for a distance-limited one — in the drawing, in the exported files, and as the label drawn on an exported document.

### Tracks and data

- Access: Main menu > Tracks and data
- Keyboard shortcut: <kbd>g</kbd> <kbd>g</kbd>
- URL path: `/#tools=import-file` (the old `track-viewer` alias still works)

Toggles the tracks and data toolbar. **Upload** opens the file import modal (URL path: `/#show=file-import`; legacy `/#show=upload-track` still works), which accepts GPX, KML, KMZ, TCX and GeoJSON files; several files can be picked or dropped at once and are merged into one view. Files can also be dropped anywhere on the map. When geodata is already shown, importing asks whether to **append** the new data or **replace** what's loaded. An external track can also be loaded directly by URL via `/#import-url=<URL>` (legacy `gpx-url=` and `load=` still work), supporting the same GPX, KML, KMZ, TCX and GeoJSON formats. Once a track is loaded, the toolbar offers:

- **Colorize by** (dropdown) — Inactive, then the modes in groups set off by dividers: Elevation, Steepness / Time, Speed, Heading / Surface, Smoothness, Road type, Track grade, the two difficulty ratings and Waymark color (these once the track has been matched to the path network) / Heart rate, Cadence, Power, Temperature / Battery, GSM signal (entries with no data in the track are disabled; every mode except Elevation, Speed and Time is premium — badged with a gem and disabled without premium access). While a mode is active, the dropdown's first item is a **Show colorizing legend** checkbox (set off by a divider) that toggles a color-scale legend below the toolbar
- a warning icon while the loaded track isn't part of a saved map — unlike drawing, a track isn't carried in the URL, so it exists only in this browser until saved via **Save to my maps**. It does survive a page reload: the track you have open is kept in the browser (IndexedDB) and put back when you reload the page you were on. That copy goes with the browser's storage, which is why the warning stays.
- **More** (⋮ menu) — gathers the remaining actions:
  - **Update elevation** — refill/override elevation from the terrain model (shown only while the track still has recorded elevation to replace); premium users get the high-resolution DEM where available
  - **Save to my maps** — save the loaded track into a new personal map (requires login; prompts to log in first otherwise). Hidden when a map is already active, since the active-map toolbar's own **Save** persists the track with the map. Older `#track-uid=…` share links still load
  - **Default style** — opens a modal to set the color, width, fill, dash, line cap/join and waypoint shape applied to imported features that carry no style of their own (independent of the drawing tool's defaults), and **Show label** (Always — the default, On hover, When selected) for the features' names. The style can also be preset via the read-only `/#track-style=` URL param (see `doc/url-params.md`)
  - **Simplify all** — thins every loaded line and polygon in place, keeping them where they are rather than converting them to anything. The dialog's slider starts on a deviation derived from the data's own density and reads out how many points it would be left with; the per-point channels and the matched path details are thinned alongside the coordinates. A surviving point carries the **average** of the sensor samples it now stands for (heart rate, cadence, power, speed, temperature) rather than just its own, since the algorithm keeps corners and those correlate with slowing down; times and bearings keep their own point's value, and elevation rides along in the coordinate untouched. Shown only while something but waypoints is loaded
  - **Convert all to drawing** — turn every loaded feature into editable drawing features (a recorded track is replaced and warns that its per-point data — elevation, heart rate, speed, time — will be dropped; the conversion dialog also asks what properties and label to give the drawing, and for a maximum deviation, offered already filled in with one derived from the track's own density — the gentlest that gets the drawing down to a few hundred editable vertices, or zero for a track short enough to convert whole)
- delete and close buttons

Clicking a line, polygon or waypoint on the map selects it and gives it a **selection toolbar** of its own, without opening the tool — a button at the head of that toolbar opens the tool's panel; data brought in by an import, a finished recording or a conversion arrives with its line already selected where it holds exactly one (several lines have no obvious pick, and data restored from a link or a reload is not selected at all). The selected feature is highlighted by a blue halo — around the line, or around the waypoint's own marker, which keeps its colors either way, and the toolbar is named by its kind — a line additionally numbered among the loaded lines — not by the feature's own name, which the Properties dialog shows. A selected line makes itself the one the elevation profile, "More info" and "Match to paths" act on; deselecting it (× or <kbd>Esc</kbd>) leaves an open profile where it is. The toolbar offers, for a line (narrower screens fold the rarer buttons into the ⋮ menu, Join first, then "More info" and Split, each with its own caret's items following it there):

- **Elevation profile** — chart of elevation along it (with total climb/descent); recorded waypoints are marked along it with their name and elevation and their distance ticked on the x-axis (toggleable), and a toolbar below the chart can download it as SVG (not in an embedded map, where the browser forbids downloads)
- **More info** — a popup with the track's distance, min/max elevation, total climb and total descent
- **Split** — cuts the line in two. Arming it makes the track offer the recorded point nearest the pointer as the cut, labelled with how far into the track it sits and how much of it is left past the cut, and the selection halo splits into two colours at it (blue up to the cut, amber past it) so which part goes where is visible before anything happens; a mouse cuts where it points, a finger places the cut first (dragging it along the track to adjust) and confirms it from the toolbar. Arming it replaces the selection toolbar with a bar saying to click the track where to cut it, a Cancel button (<kbd>Esc</kbd> does the same) and — once a finger has placed a cut — the button confirming it. Every other toolbar and floating window goes away for as long as it is armed, the way picking a home location or a photo's position clears the screen; the difference is that the features stay live, since a click on one is what the mode is waiting for. The cut is always an existing point of the track, which both halves keep, so nothing is interpolated and the per-point channels (times, heart rate, cadence) are cut alongside the coordinates. Only the selected line answers the cut, so every other feature stays selectable while the mode is armed
- **Split into segments** (the Split button's caret) — breaks a multi-segment recording (a segment per recorder pause) into a feature per segment; offered only for a line that has more than one
- **Join** — joins the selected track with another loaded one. It is a menu of the two ways of putting them together — into one line, or keeping a segment each (a `MultiLineString`, drawn with a gap rather than a straight line across the break) — since neither is the one a press could be assumed to mean; picking one arms the join: the selection toolbar makes way for a bar saying to click the track to join with, alongside a Cancel button (<kbd>Esc</kbd> does the same), every other toolbar and floating window goes away as it does for a split, and the next track clicked is joined onto the selected one, lighting up as it is pointed at so the pair is visible before anything happens. Recorded times decide which track comes first and are never made to run backwards; without them the pairing of endpoints with the smallest gap wins, so either track may be turned round. The per-point channels (times, heart rate, cadence), the path details and the elevation credits of both come along — a channel only one of them recorded is left empty over the other's points — and the selected track's own name, style and metadata carry over to the result, its name with the other's appended. Offered only while more than one line is loaded; Escape leaves the mode
- **Match to paths** (⋮ menu) — snap the track onto GraphHopper's routing graph. It asks one thing: which transport carried it, since nothing in a GPX says. The matched line replaces the track and carries the path details the router valued it by, which is what makes Surface, Smoothness, Road type, Track grade and the difficulty ratings available for a recording; it also removes GPS wander, so the distance stops being inflated. The matched line has its own points, so the track's timestamps and recorded sensor data (heart rate, cadence, speed) are lost — the modal warns when the track actually has any. Each recorded segment is matched separately, since GraphHopper reads a file as one sequence and would otherwise route across a pause; a segment it cannot get through is left as recorded rather than failing the whole track. It declines rather than lying when the result comes back meaningfully longer than the recording — matching can only answer with paths that exist, so a stretch taken across open ground, or by a transport the chosen profile cannot follow, comes back routed *around* it: believable, and not where anyone went. A track that changes transport partway (a walk, then the drive home, with the recorder never stopped) cannot be matched at all and has to be split first
- **Simplify** (⋮ menu) — thins that one line in place, leaving the rest of the loaded data alone; the same dialog as the tool menu's "Simplify all"
- **Convert to drawing** (⋮ menu) — turns that one line into editable drawing features and leaves the rest of the loaded data alone, asking the same conversion dialog as the tool menu's "Convert all"

Every selected feature, whatever its kind, also carries **Properties** — the same editor the drawing tool uses: the name shown on the map, a table of the feature's own data (a row per key and value, added and removed by hand), and the style it is drawn with (color, width, fill, dash, line cap/join for a line or polygon; color, shape and icon for a waypoint). A line whose ends meet can be switched between line and polygon. The style is written into the feature's own properties, in our `freemap:*` form and in simplestyle alike, so it survives an export and reads back the same — and the feature then stops following the tool's **Default style**. Labels here are plain text: the drawing tool's `{p:key}` placeholders are not drawn.

A polygon gets that menu without the matching, and a waypoint an ⋮ **Actions** menu — convert to drawing, the map context menu's whole list for the waypoint, and an **Open in…** submenu. The 🗑 button (<kbd>Del</kbd>) deletes just that feature, leaving the rest of the loaded data; the last one deleted clears the view. An edited collection no longer matches the file it came from, so it stops being re-fetched from `track-uid=`/`import-url=` and becomes this browser's own copy (kept in IndexedDB, and saved with an active map).
